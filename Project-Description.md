# PillVision

## Project Description

PillVision is a senior design project focused on developing a computer vision-based pill counting and verification system for a pharmacy-related setting.

The project will investigate methods for accurately counting pills under realistic conditions, including cases where pills are touching, partially overlapping, or clustered. Controlled minimal vibration may be used to reduce severe clustering while allowing the computer vision system to handle remaining touching and partially overlapping pills.

The team is currently keeping the camera configuration flexible. The project may begin with a simple camera setup and later evaluate whether additional camera viewpoints provide meaningful improvements in counting accuracy.

The project may also explore methods for detecting pills that appear visually inconsistent with the expected group, such as incorrect, broken, or damaged pills.

---

## Topic Statement

### Context

The intended application area for PillVision is pill counting in pharmacy-related environments, where multiple pills may need to be counted accurately and efficiently.

### Problem Statement

Pills may be positioned close together, touching, partially overlapping, or clustered, which can make computer vision-based counting more challenging. A system that depends on complete mechanical separation of every pill may also require additional hardware and complexity.

### Significance

PillVision will investigate whether accurate pill counting can be achieved while reducing the amount of physical separation required. The significance and limitations of existing pill-counting approaches will be researched further before this section is finalized.

### Proposed Direction

The team proposes developing a computer vision-based system that detects and counts pills from camera images. Controlled minimal vibration may be used to reduce severe clustering, while vision algorithms will be used to handle remaining touching and partially overlapping pills. The team may also investigate visual verification features for identifying pills that differ from the expected group.
